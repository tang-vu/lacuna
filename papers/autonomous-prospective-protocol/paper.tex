\begin{abstract}
Literature-based discovery methods are often evaluated retrospectively on a small set of known
examples, creating opportunities for case selection, parameter adaptation, and hindsight leakage.
This protocol defines a no-human prospective benchmark for one narrower task: ranking pairs of
Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) that will later acquire direct co-indexing links in complete annual
PubMed baselines. The 2026 T0 source, exhaustive score-free candidate universe, metric contract,
and predictions were hash-sealed before any T1 outcome source existed. T0 contains 39,994,988
distinct PubMed identifiers and 31,110 MeSH descriptors. Deterministic eligibility rules produced
7,310,895 exact-zero candidate pairs. A fixed-point Adamic--Adar score and five baselines were then
computed for every candidate and placed in immutable total orders. An automated watcher accepts
only sequential official 2027--2029 PubMed and MeSH release identities. Registered outcome,
integrity, power, performance, and structural-diagnostic gates resolve mechanically to pass, fail,
or abstain after the three-release window. No prospective outcome currently exists: the benchmark
is \texttt{not\_ready}, the formula is unvalidated, and validated knowledge-gap pairs number zero.
Even a future pass would validate only PubMed/MeSH link-emergence ranking, not knowledge-gap
detection, scientific novelty, causality, importance, or autonomous discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Literature-based discovery (LBD) searches for relationships that can be inferred by connecting
separate bodies of published work. Swanson's fish-oil and Raynaud's-syndrome case established the
canonical example \cite{swanson1986}. Subsequent studies replicated, extended, and automated such
ABC-style searches \cite{gordon1996,weeber2001,preiss2017}. These studies also illustrate a hard
evaluation problem: a method can be adjusted after its famous target and historical outcome are
already known.

Temporal link prediction offers an objective but deliberately narrower alternative. A graph is
constructed at T0, a complete ordering is sealed, and edges newly observed at a later T1 are used
as outcomes. Adamic--Adar is a standard local link-prediction score that discounts high-degree
common neighbours \cite{adamic2003}. This benchmark applies that family to maintained-current MeSH
assignments in annual PubMed baselines. It does not claim that later co-indexing proves a new
scientific relationship. The measured event is database-link emergence.

The project that hosts this benchmark, \emph{lacuna}, previously tested two OpenAlex topic-level
metrics against a pre-registered Swanson reproduction. Both failed. Those negative results remain
published and were not used as evidence that the active formula works. The present protocol was
frozen as a separate prospective track before its candidate scores were inspected.

\section{Scope and claim boundary}

The primary estimand is ranking performance for future direct MeSH co-indexing links in complete
PubMed baselines. A pair's rank does not estimate whether the relationship is true, causal,
important, clinically useful, mechanistic, novel to humanity, or absent from existing knowledge.
PubMed indexes academic biomedical literature and does not cover all academic work, much less
craft, practitioner, indigenous, historical, humanities, or other non-academic knowledge.

The allowed positive conclusion after a pass is therefore only: the sealed formula was validated
for prospective PubMed/MeSH link-emergence ranking under this protocol. ``Validated knowledge-gap
detector'', ``autonomous scientific discovery'', and equivalent claims are outside the design.

\section{Sealing chronology and materials}

The machine-readable protocol first appeared on 12 August 2026. The complete T0 source, score-free
candidate contract, exhaustive candidate universe, formula, and full prediction order were sealed
in successive public repository commits on 13 August 2026. A source-pinned release watcher was
frozen on 24 August 2026. The later registration packet records this already sealed state; it is not
represented as a new preregistration.

T0 comprises 1,334 official 2026 PubMed baseline transports with 54,267,874,919 compressed bytes
and 39,994,988 distinct parsed PubMed identifiers, plus the matching official MeSH XML transport
with 31,110 descriptors. The official distribution locations are maintained by the U.S. National
Library of Medicine \cite{nlmpubmed,nlmmesh}. Every source filename, URL, byte length, SHA-256
digest, record subtotal, and descriptor-vocabulary identity is pinned. PubMed/MeSH assignments are
maintained-current indexing, not period-appropriate historical indexing.

Large source corpora and computed binaries remain outside the Git repository. Small manifests pin
their exact content, dimensions, and formats. Structural audits run from a clean clone; full
byte-level replay additionally requires the pinned local files.

\section{Score-free candidate universe}

All unordered descriptor pairs $(u,v)$ were considered in lexicographic descriptor-UI order. A pair
was eligible when both endpoints occurred in at least 100 T0 articles, its independence expectation
was at least 5, and its exact T0 direct co-indexing count was zero. Identical descriptors,
ancestor--descendant pairs, and pairs sharing an entry term were excluded. Sampling was forbidden.

The reducer processed all 39,994,988 distinct PMIDs and measured 342,733,118 descriptor
assignments and 51,128,229 distinct positive descriptor-pair rows. The resulting exhaustive stream
contains 7,310,895 candidates. Candidate construction did not compute a metric, score, rank, or
prediction, and it used no human or LLM labels.

An exact-zero value here means only that no direct pair row occurred in this maintained-current T0
index. It is not evidence that the underlying relationship or knowledge is absent.

\section{Frozen graph and ranking methods}

Let $N$ be the 39,994,988-record denominator, $n_i$ the exact support of descriptor $i$, and
$c_{ij}$ the exact direct co-indexing count. An unweighted positive-association backbone contains
edge $(i,j)$ exactly when
\begin{equation}
  c_{ij}N > n_i n_j.
\end{equation}
Equality is excluded. Integer products use at least 128 unsigned bits. The sealed backbone has
31,760,211 undirected edges.

For candidate endpoints $u$ and $v$, let $\Gamma(u)$ and $\Gamma(v)$ denote their backbone
neighbours and $d(w)$ the unweighted degree of common neighbour $w$. The primary fixed-point score
is
\begin{equation}
 S_{AA}(u,v) = \sum_{w \in \Gamma(u)\cap\Gamma(v)}
 \left\lfloor\frac{2^{48}}{\ln d(w)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Decimal logarithms and division use 80-digit precision with round-half-even before flooring; the
sum is checked as an unsigned 64-bit integer. Missing, non-finite, or overflowing values force
abstention without a prediction seal.

Five baselines were frozen with the primary formula: endpoint prevalence $n_un_v$, common-neighbour
count, resource allocation $\sum 2^{48}/d(w)$ with per-term flooring, exact-rational Jaccard
similarity, and preferential attachment $d(u)d(v)$. Every ordering uses a deterministic hash-based
tie key followed by the pair key, with no labels, descriptor names, or future outcomes involved.

The completed run emitted one score tuple for every eligible pair. Of 7,310,895 primary scores,
7,310,826 were nonzero. Source, formula, dependency, compiler, binary, conformance, input, output,
and order hashes are sealed. These are computation-integrity measurements from an unvalidated
method, not prospective results.

\section{Prospective outcome acquisition}

A monthly machine watcher observes only the next sequential official release identity for 2027,
2028, and 2029. It cannot overwrite an observed release. Missing, skipped, out-of-order, partial, or
conflicting evidence forces abstention. API result pages are not accepted as baselines. Evaluation
waits for a complete official 2029 PubMed baseline and its matching MeSH release; publication dates
are reported but do not define database emergence.

A new row is a PMID--descriptor-pair row absent from T0 and present at T1. A positive outcome
requires at least three distinct new co-indexed PMIDs spanning at least two journals and two
publication years. A zero count is labelled only ``no observed link emergence in the window''.
One or two PMIDs, a retired or ambiguously mapped descriptor, failed source reconciliation, or a
qualifying retraction makes the pair censored. Re-indexing of pre-existing PMIDs remains a database
link event but is counted separately. Exact PMIDs and source rows must accompany every outcome.

\section{Registered evaluation}

The primary population contains all non-censored eligible T0 pairs. Fewer than 200 positive or
20,000 negative outcomes triggers abstention for insufficient power. Otherwise, every one of the
following gates must pass:
\begin{itemize}
  \item precision at 100 is at least 0.25;
  \item precision-at-100 lift over prevalence is at least 5;
  \item average-precision lift over prevalence is at least 3; and
  \item the 95\% bootstrap lower bound on precision-at-100 lift is at least 2, using 10,000
        replicates with the frozen seed \texttt{lacuna-autonomous-prospective-v1}.
\end{itemize}

Two automatic structural diagnostics are also gates rather than negative ground truth. Excluded
immediate-parent siblings may occupy at most 1\% of the primary top 1\%; a deterministic
degree-matched distant cohort may occupy at most 5\% of the primary top 5\%. The final state is pass
only if all source, integrity, power, primary-metric, and structural gates pass. Any evaluable
primary or structural failure is terminal for the formula. Insufficient source, integrity,
outcome, or power evidence yields abstention, without manual override. A later formula requires a
new protocol and new T0 seal.

\section{Limitations}

The target is an indexing event rather than scientific truth. MeSH practices and descriptor
identity can change between releases, and later indexing of old articles can create an observed
link without a newly published relationship. The protocol reports that component separately but
does not remove it from the database-event target. Requiring multiple PMIDs, journals, and years
reduces single-record noise but may miss real low-volume links.

Local link-prediction measures can favour structural proximity, degree patterns, and ontology
artifacts. Frozen baselines and structural diagnostics expose some of this behaviour but cannot
establish mechanism or utility. The metric was selected before scoring but is not independently
validated. A single future pass, if obtained, would be specific to the frozen data model, outcome
definition, and release window.

The project preserves negative results, generated state, and measured state with separate labels.
This supports traceability and FAIR research-software practice \cite{barker2022}, but hash-pinned
availability does not by itself guarantee long-term hosting of every large source or binary file.
